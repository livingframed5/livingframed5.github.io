## Part 2 — The Five-Part Prompt (Recap)

You learned this in Lesson 0. Here it is in action, three times, with real concrete jobs.

### Example A — a bid cover email

> ROLE: "You are a bid writer for a residential concrete contractor."
> TASK: "Draft a short email to send this quote to the client."
> CONTEXT: "Driveway + front walk, 1,900 sq ft, broom finish, stamped border. Quote total
> $14,200. They're a homeowner who cares about on-time start and clean finish."
> FORMAT: "Three short paragraphs, warm but professional, sign it 'Mike, A+ Concrete'."
> CHECK: "Tell me if I should include any dates or details you think are missing."

### Example B — a "sorry we're behind" email

> ROLE: "You are a construction office manager."
> TASK: "Draft a polite email telling a client their patio pour is delayed one week due to rain."
> CONTEXT: "Job was scheduled for Thursday; forecast is heavy rain Wed–Thu; we'll pour Monday
> if weather holds. We reschedule, never cancel. Client was already anxious about timing."
> FORMAT: "Short, apologetic but confident, give the new date and a phone number."
> CHECK: "Keep it under 90 words."

### Example C — a site note → clean record

> ROLE: "You are a project note-taker."
> TASK: "Turn my messy field notes into a clean job log."
> CONTEXT: "Here are my notes: [paste your notes]. Job = Henderson foundation, 8-11am, crew 4,
> rebar in place, inspector due Friday, one truck late 40 min, need extra 2 yds of 4000 mix."
> FORMAT: "A dated log with sections: What happened / Crew / Materials / Issues / Next steps."
> CHECK: "Ask me anything that's unclear instead of guessing."
