# Lesson 5 — AI Guardrails, Privacy & Safety

**Time:** ~30 minutes
**Tools:** none — this lesson is about judgment and habits.

> **Why this lesson exists:** AI tools make mistakes, and they remember everything you give them.
> For a concrete contractor, the two real risks are (1) an AI draft that sounds right but has the
> wrong numbers in a bid you sign, and (2) client/employee/cost data you didn't mean to share.
> This lesson is short, but it's the one that keeps you safe. Read it before you paste anything
> sensitive from Lessons 1–4.

---

## What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Decide what is and isn't safe to put into an AI tool.
- Check AI output before it goes out the door (the 3-point verification).
- Protect client, employee, and pricing data in everyday use.
- Set simple rules for your crew and office so AI is used right by everyone.

---

## Part 1 — What NOT to Put Into an AI Tool

Three things never go into a free AI chat:

| Never paste | Why | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Full signed contracts / agreements** | They contain both sides' business data and possibly personal info (names, DOB, addresses, tax numbers) | Paste only the *clause or paragraph* you're asking about; redact names/addresses; or use your platform's contract tooling |
| **Bank/credit-card statements with account numbers** | Direct financial access risk | Remove account numbers & payee details you don't need; use categories only |
| **Client personal info beyond what's needed** (SSN, DOB, tax IDs, medical notes) | Data-privacy exposure; you may be legally required to protect it | Describe the situation without the identifiers: "residential client, 2-story house, payment 25 days late" |

**Also be careful with:**
- **Your pricing sheets & rate cards.** Treat them as confidential. If you're worried, don't paste
  them into free consumer tools — or use a business/paid account and delete the chat after.
- **Job-site photos of people** or private property if you're using them for marketing. Get the
  owner's OK before publishing faces or recognizable houses.
- **Employee details** (pay rates, disciplinary notes, medical info). Describe the situation, not
  the person's file.

> **The test:** before you paste, ask *"Would I want this on a public billboard with my name on
> it?"* If the answer is no, redact it first.

---

## Part 2 — The 3-Point Verification (before anything leaves the office)

AI output is a *draft*, not a fact. Before any AI draft goes to a client, a GC, a bank, or a
post, run the 3-point check:

1. **Numbers check.** Recompute every number by hand or calculator. A bid with a wrong total is
   a legal problem, not an AI problem. AI does not get a pass because it sounded confident.
2. **Facts check.** Every date, name, price, psi, spec, and term must come from YOUR records.
   Cross it against the job file. Anything the AI "assumed" gets confirmed or deleted.
3. **Tone & fit check.** Does this sound like you/your company? Take out anything stiff or
   generic. A client can tell when a contractor pasted boilerplate.

**The habit:** *"AI drafts, you decide"* — and deciding means checking, not just approving.

---

## Part 3 — When Things Go Wrong (worst cases & fixes)

**The AI gave me a wrong quantity and I used it.** Fix it *before* the client sees it — run the
verification. If it already went out: correct it fast and honestly, same as you'd fix any
estimate error. Own it; it happens to everyone. The rule is you check before you send.

**The AI "hallucinated" a code, product, or spec.** This is common with anything niche. Never
trust AI for: local building codes, chemical/mix data, SDS/MSDS, safety regs, or warranty rules.
**Always verify against the actual source** (the code book, the SDS, the manufacturer's sheet).

**A client asks for AI-generated content.** No rule against using AI in your bid — but don't let
a client think a human personally analyzed their job if that's not true. Be honest about how you
estimated. Transparency builds trust.

**A client sends you data (drawings, their pricing, their plans).** Treat it as confidential.
Don't paste their proprietary documents into free tools either — same rule as your own.

## Part 4 — Simple Rules for Your Crew & Office

If your team uses AI too (and they will), set expectations with a one-page policy. Here's a
starter you can adapt:

> **Company AI policy (draft):**
> 1. AI drafts; a person reviews before anything is sent or published. No exception for bids,
>    emails to clients, or financial documents.
> 2. Never paste client contracts, bank statements, tax IDs, Social Security numbers, or full
>    employee files into AI tools. Redact first.
> 3. Never let AI decide safety, quality, or legal matters. Those need a person.
> 4. Don't publish job-site photos of people or identifiable homes without the owner's OK.
> 5. Don't use free consumer AI for company pricing sheets or rate cards — keep those internal.
> 6. If in doubt, ask the office before you paste anything.

Give your crew the *do/don't* list, not a lecture. Print it or keep it in the office group chat.

---

## Part 5 — The Standing Rules (the whole lesson in 6 lines)

1. **AI drafts, you decide.** Never skip the check.
2. **Real numbers only.** AI never invents a dollar, a yard, or a date you didn't give it.
3. **Redact before you paste.** Names, account numbers, prices, contracts, employee files.
4. **Source of truth wins.** Codes, SDS, specs, contracts, and regulations come from the real
   document — always verify against it.
5. **People and law decide.** Safety, quality, hiring, and legal matters stay human.
6. **Set rules for the crew.** One short policy beats a thousand "don'ts."
