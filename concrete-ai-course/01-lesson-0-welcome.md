# Lesson 0 — Welcome & How to Use This Module

**Time:** ~10 minutes
**Tools needed:** nothing yet — just read

---

## Welcome to the AI Module

If you run a concrete business — flatwork, foundations, driveways, stamped, ready-mix — you've
got two things in short supply: **time** and **good information**. AI won't pour the concrete.
But it will take the busywork off your plate: the drafting, the emailing, the quoting roughs,
the note-taking, the chasing. That's what this module is for.

This lesson is short. It answers three questions:

1. What is AI, in 30 seconds, without the hype?
2. What is AI actually good at (and bad at) for a concrete business?
3. What is the one skill you'll reuse in every other lesson of this module?

---

## Part 1 — What AI Is, in 30 Seconds

AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Google Gemini are **autocomplete engines on steroids**. They've
read a huge amount of text and learned to predict what a good response looks like. When you give
them a clear question or task, they generate a useful draft back.

That's it. They're not "thinking." They're producing the most-likely-useful answer. The practical
implication for you:

- **They're fast** — a draft bid email in 15 seconds, not 20 minutes.
- **They're helpful at starting** — the hardest part of any document is the blank page.
- **They're wrong sometimes** — confident-sounding mistakes. That's why Lesson 5 exists.

---

## Part 2 — What AI Is Good At (for a Concrete Business)

| AI is genuinely good at | AI is NOT good at |
|---|---|
| Drafting text: bids, emails, marketing, job descriptions | Making final decisions — you're still the boss |
| Summarizing: meeting notes, emails, specs, long docs | Getting real numbers right without you checking |
| First-draft estimates and rough quantities from YOUR inputs | Estimating from a photo without calibration |
| Explaining things in plain English (contracts, codes) | Anything involving current, exact, local facts (prices, permits, weather) unless you give it the facts |
| Brainstorming and options | Judging a human being (client, employee) |
| Converting notes → clean documents | Working with no information — garbage in, garbage out |
| Standardizing: same format every time | Replacing your experience and your license |

The golden rule of this entire module:

> **AI drafts, you decide.**

## Part 3 — The One Skill You'll Reuse Everywhere

Every prompt in every lesson follows the same five-part pattern:

| Part | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ROLE** | Who the AI should pretend to be | "You are an experienced concrete estimator." |
| **TASK** | What you want it to do | "Create a first-draft material list for this job." |
| **CONTEXT** | The facts it needs | "6,000 sq ft, 4-in. slab, rebar at 18-in. centers, 4,000 psi." |
| **FORMAT** | How the answer should look | "Return a table with item, quantity, unit." |
| **CHECK** | How you'll verify | "Flag anything you assumed and I'll confirm it." |

### The pattern in one sentence
> **ROLE + TASK + CONTEXT + FORMAT + CHECK** — give the AI a job, the facts, the shape of the
> answer, and a way to stay honest. Do that and you'll beat 90% of users who just type a question.

### A quick example you'll reuse forever — the "summarize this" prompt

> "You are my office assistant. Summarize the attached email chain in 3 bullet points: what the
> client wants, any dates, and what they're asking me to do. If anything is unclear, say 'UNSURE'
> next to it. Keep it under 60 words."

## Try It Yourself (2 minutes, optional)

Open any free AI tool (ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini). Paste this:

> "You are a friendly explainer. Explain to a concrete contractor in 5 sentences why an AI draft
> can be wrong even when it sounds confident. Use one example about concrete quantities."

See how it answers. Notice the tone: confident. That's exactly why you'll always CHECK.

## Quick Reference Card — the 5-Part Prompt

```
ROLE    →  You are a [job title].
TASK    →  Do [one specific thing].
CONTEXT →  Here are the facts: [numbers, names, dates, specs].
FORMAT  →  Answer as [table / bullets / email / 1 page].
CHECK   →  List anything you assumed; I'll confirm before I use it.
```
