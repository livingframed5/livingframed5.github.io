# Lesson 3 — Job Site & Operations AI

**Time:** ~45 minutes
**Tools:** A chat AI app on your phone (voice/text) + your regular scheduling tools
(calendar, spreadsheets, dispatch board).

---

## What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Turn messy daily site updates into clean job logs and client updates.
- Plan pours and schedules with AI (crews, trucks, equipment) — and know what AI can't know.
- Use AI to turn photos into QA/safety notes and field documentation.
- Set up simple early-warning checks (weather, ordering, deadlines) so you're not surprised.

---

## Part 1 — The Daily Site Log (Stop Re-Writing Everything)

Every day you say "same as yesterday" — then re-type it anyway. AI fixes that.

**Workflow:**
1. On site (or driving), dictate a messy update: *"Henderson job, slab down by 9, crew 4, one
   truck late, chip a broom edge near the garage door, need sealer Friday, inspector came and
   said fine."*
2. Paste/voice it into your AI app with this prompt:

> "Turn this site update into a clean daily job log with sections: Job, Date, Crew on site,
> Work done, Materials/mix used, Issues & notes, Next steps. Keep it factual, under 100 words,
> plain English. Flag anything I need to act on. Here's the update: [paste]."

3. Copy the result into your job log / email to the GC / field report. Done in 2 minutes.

> **Pro tip:** keep a single "site notes" chat per active job in your AI app. Every day, paste the
> new update in the same chat and ask for the *updated* log. The app remembers the job's history,
> so the log gets more useful over time.

## Part 2 — Pour & Crew Planning (The Realistic Version)

AI is genuinely useful at *organizing* a pour plan — and genuinely bad at *guessing your crew's
real capacity or the site's real constraints.* So: let AI draft the plan, you fix the facts.

> "You are a construction scheduler. Build a draft pour plan for this job:
> - 3,200 sq ft slab, 4-in. thick, needs ~16 yd of 4,000 psi mix.
> - Crew: 5 finishers/laborers; 1 pump available; ready-mix plant 20 min away.
> - Weather: forecast rain Thursday, otherwise clear.
> - Inspector required before pour; inspector usually books 48h ahead.
> Return a day-by-day schedule: prep, inspection, pour day (times), finishing, cure, strip/edges,
> sealing. Mark anything that depends on an assumption as 'CONFIRM'."

Then you overrule the parts you know better: your finisher count, your real pump capacity, the
plant's Saturday hours, that one client whose yard can't take a truck. AI organizes; you direct.

**A note on weather tools:** your AI app isn't a weather service. Use your real weather app for
the forecast, and feed the forecast INTO the AI as context. Never let the AI invent the forecast.

## Part 3 — Photos → Field Documentation (QA, Safety, Punch Lists)

Your phone's photos are already documentation; AI makes them searchable.

**Photo → QA note:**

> "Describe this photo factually for a QA record: what's poured, finish quality visible, any
> defects you can see (cracking, honeycombing, edge damage), and anything that looks like it
> needs follow-up. Say 'CAN'T VERIFY' for anything unclear. Do not guess."

**Photo → safety note:**

> "Look at this job site photo. List any obvious safety hazards you can see (trip hazards, rebar
> exposures, PPE missing, standing water on the slab, guardrails). Only list things clearly
> visible. If nothing is clear, say so."

> **Honest limitations:** AI can *describe what's in the frame* but can't judge if a crack is
> structural, can't smell diesel, can't hear a pump running rough. Use it for documentation and
> reminders — a human still makes the call on safety and quality.

**Punch list from a walkthrough:** dictate a rapid-fire list while walking the job, then:

> "Turn this punch list into a numbered list grouped by area, with a checkbox next to each item,
> and flag the 3 items most likely to delay final. Items: [paste your dictated list]."

## Part 4 — Early-Warning Checks (Stop Being Surprised)

Build a simple morning routine using your AI app + calendar:

> "Here is my schedule today: [paste 3–5 jobs/times]. Here's today's forecast: [paste from your
> weather app]. List: (1) any weather conflicts, (2) anything due today (orders, inspections,
> calls), (3) the top 2 things I shouldn't let slip. Keep it under 100 words."

Run this each morning before the first pour. It takes 60 seconds and turns three apps' worth of
information into one prioritized list.

**Other quick wins:**
- **Order reminders:** "I need to order sealer for the Henderson job Friday. Remind me how much
  for 3,200 sq ft at 2 coats, and what I should double-check with the supplier."
- **New-hire on-call answers:** create a simple FAQ from your own practices so a new employee can
  ask the AI instead of interrupting you:
  > "You are my crew's job-site assistant. Answer based ONLY on these facts I give you: [your
  > practices: arrival time, PPE rules, cell phone rules, who to call for issues]. If a question
  > isn't covered, say 'NOT IN MY NOTES — ask the office.'"

## Try It Yourself (7 minutes)

1. Dictate a real (or made-up) site update and turn it into a clean log with the Part 1 prompt.
2. Plan one real upcoming pour with the Part 2 prompt — then write down 3 things you'd change
   that AI couldn't have known.
3. Take a photo of a finished (or in-progress) slab and run the QA prompt on it.
