# Lesson 4 — Business & Finance AI

**Time:** ~45 minutes
**Tools:** A chat AI app + your existing tools (invoicing software, bookkeeping files, bank
statements). AI won't replace your bookkeeper or your accounting software — it'll make both
faster and help you finally understand your numbers.

> **Read this first — the numbers rule:** AI is *excellent* at organizing and explaining numbers
> YOU give it, and *dangerous* when asked to make numbers up. It cannot log in to your bank, it
> cannot know your real costs, and it will happily produce a "cash flow forecast" from thin air.
> That's fiction, not planning. **Rule: every number in, every number out is YOUR data.** AI
> formats, categorizes, drafts, and explains — it never invents a dollar.

---

## What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Draft invoices, payment reminders, and collection emails that actually get answered.
- Turn bank/credit-card exports into tidy, organized records for your bookkeeper.
- Explain your own P&L and cash flow in plain English (finally).
- Run a "cash runway" check and a simple monthly review using AI + your real numbers.
- Write job descriptions and job ads for concrete workers without starting from zero.

## Part 1 — Invoicing & Getting Paid (the money you already earned)

**Faster invoice drafts:** your invoicing software handles the actual invoice. Use AI to write
the *cover communication* that gets it paid on time.

> "You are an accounts-receivable assistant for a concrete contractor. Write a short, professional
> email to send with this invoice: Invoice #[...] to [client], amount $[x], for [job/scope].
> Terms: net 15. Tone: friendly, clear, no pressure. Mention a thank-you for the work. Under
> 80 words."

**Payment reminders — escalate politely:**

> "Draft a polite first payment reminder for invoice #[...], $[x], 5 days past due, client
> [name]. Tone: assume it's an oversight, friendly, under 70 words."

> "Draft a firmer final reminder for invoice #[...], $[x], 25 days past due. Reference our net-15
> terms and ask for payment or a call by Friday. Professional, not aggressive, under 80 words."

**Collections script (for the phone call you're dreading):**

> "Write a 45-second phone script for a concrete contractor calling about a 30-day-past-due
> invoice for $[x] with client [name]. The contractor wants to sound professional, keep the
> relationship, and get a payment date. Include what to say if the client says they can't pay
> this month."

> **Habit:** batch it. Every Monday, paste your week's unpaid invoices into one prompt and get all
> your reminders drafted in one go. You'll stop avoiding collections.

## Part 2 — Books in a Fraction of the Time

Your bank and card statements are full of your actual spending. AI is a great organizer — it can't
log in, so *you* export the CSV and paste it.

**Monthly export cleanup:**

> "You are a bookkeeping assistant. I'm giving you a CSV of my business transactions for
> [month]. Categorize each line into: Materials, Equipment & rentals, Labor/subcontractors,
> Fuel & vehicles, Tools, Insurance, Office/software, Marketing, Taxes & licenses, Owner draws,
> Other. Keep the original rows; add a Category column. Flag any line that's unclear so I can
> review it. Data: [paste CSV]."

**Then use it for:** giving your bookkeeper a clean, categorized file (saves their time = saves
you money), or reviewing where money actually goes.

> **Privacy before you paste:** this is bank data. Read Lesson 5 first — remove account numbers,
> and don't paste this into a free tool unless you're comfortable. When in doubt, use a business
> account and a paid tool, or have your bookkeeper do it.

**Explain your numbers to yourself:**

> "Explain this in plain English for a busy business owner. Here's my income and expense summary
> for last quarter: [paste numbers]. Tell me: (1) what my gross margin is, (2) the 3 biggest cost
> categories, (3) one thing that looks worth investigating, (4) one simple question I should ask
> my bookkeeper. Keep it under 150 words. Do not invent numbers."

## Part 3 — Cash Flow: Runway Check, Not Fiction

A simple, honest cash check you can run in 5 minutes with real numbers.

> "You are a small-business cash advisor. Using ONLY these real numbers I give you, tell me my
> cash position and rough runway:
> - Cash in bank today: $[x]
> - Expected income this month (invoices due): $[x]
> - Known bills this month: payroll $[x], materials/credit card $[x], rent/insurance $[x],
>   equipment payments $[x], other $[x].
> - Approximate monthly burn: $[x]
> Show: month-end projected balance, how many months I could run at this burn with no new income,
> and the 2 biggest levers to improve it. If any number is missing, say 'MISSING' — do not guess."

This is the difference between planning and guessing: **every figure in your prompt is a real one
from your records.** The AI just does the arithmetic and tells you plainly how close to the edge
you are.

---

## Part 4 — Monthly Review (the 20-minute habit)

Once a month, do this with your actual exports:

> "Here are my real numbers for [month]: income $[x], expenses by category [paste]. Compare to my
> target/typical month [$[x] income, $[x] materials, etc. if you have it]. In 5 bullet points,
> tell me: what changed, the biggest cost surprise, whether we hit target, and 2 actions to take
> next month. Flag anything that doesn't match my history as 'CHECK'."

Do it on the 1st of the month, and you'll know your business better than most owners — because
most owners never sit down with their own numbers organized.

## Part 5 — Hire Help (Job Posts & Onboarding)

AI writes the ad; you write the truth about the job.

> "You are a recruiter for a concrete contractor. Write a job ad for a concrete finisher:
> - Duties: form, place, finish, cure flatwork; stamp and exposed aggregate experience a plus.
> - Requirements: 3+ years finishing, own tools (trowels, floats), valid license/insurance,
>   reliable transport, must pass drug screen.
> - Pay: $[x]/hr DOE, weekly pay, benefits [list]. Job site location varies within [counties].
> Format: clear sections, no fluff, under 150 words, written so a good finisher wants to call."

**Onboarding FAQ for a new hire (same as the crew-assistant prompt in Lesson 3):**

> "Here are my company practices for new crew: [arrival, PPE, cell phones, breaks, who to call
> for what, truck/driver rules]. Create a one-page 'Day One' sheet a new hire can read in
> 5 minutes. Plain English, friendly but clear."

1. Draft one real payment-reminder email for an invoice you're currently waiting on.
2. Export your latest bank/CC statement (or use 10 sample lines) and run the categorize prompt.
   Review the category flags.
3. Run the cash-runway prompt with real numbers. Write down ONE action it surfaced.

## Quick Reference Card — Finance Prompts

```
INVOICE   → "Draft an email to send with invoice #[..] for $[x]. Friendly, net-[n] terms, <80 words."
REMINDER  → "Polite first reminder, $[x], [n] days late. Under 70 words." (escalate from friendly → firm)
COLLECT   → "45-second phone script for a [n]-day-past-due invoice; include the 'can't pay this month' reply."
CATEGORIZE→ "Categorize this CSV into [your categories]; keep rows; add Category column; flag unclear lines."
P&L PLAIN → "Explain this P&L in plain English: margin, top 3 costs, one thing to investigate."
RUNWAY    → "Real numbers only. Show projected balance, months of runway, 2 biggest levers. Say MISSING, never guess."
REVIEW    → "Monthly numbers vs target. What changed, biggest surprise, 2 actions."
```
