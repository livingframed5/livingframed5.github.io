# Lesson 1 — Everyday AI Tools (The Basics)

**Time:** ~45 minutes
**Tools:** ChatGPT / Claude / Google Gemini (free tiers are fine) + Google's free "Gemini" or
Microsoft Copilot if you live in email/Word.

---

## What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Set up and use a chat AI tool from your phone or laptop.
- Write bids, emails, site notes, and marketing copy in minutes using the 5-part prompt.
- Use AI to summarize long emails and meeting notes instead of reading everything twice.
- Turn a voice note into a clean document (even while driving between jobs — hands-free).

---

## Part 1 — Meet Your Tools (and Which to Pick)

You don't need to be a tech person. All you need is a free account and a browser or app.

| Tool | Best for | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **ChatGPT** (chatgpt.com) | All-around drafting, reasoning, file uploads | Free tier is plenty |
| **Claude** (claude.ai) | Long documents, clear writing, careful answers | Free tier is plenty |
| **Google Gemini** (gemini.google.com) | Ties into Gmail/Google Docs, search smarts | Free tier is plenty |
| **Microsoft Copilot** (in Word/Outlook) | Already in your Microsoft apps | Free/Included |

**My recommendation for a contractor:** pick **one** chat app and get good at it. Any of the three
big ones works. Switching apps later is easy because the skill transfers — the prompts are the same.

> **Tool note:** tools update constantly and screenshots age fast. Don't chase versions — chase
> the *method*. Every tool here can do the tasks in this lesson today.

## Part 2 — The Five-Part Prompt (Recap)

You learned this in Lesson 0. Here it is in action, three times, with real concrete jobs.

### Example A — a bid cover email

> ROLE: "You are a bid writer for a residential concrete contractor."
> TASK: "Draft a short email to send this quote to the client."
> CONTEXT: "Driveway + front walk, 1,900 sq ft, broom finish, stamped border. Quote total
> $14,200. They're a homeowner who cares about on-time start and clean finish."
> FORMAT: "Three short paragraphs, warm but professional, sign it 'Mike, A+ Concrete'."
> CHECK: "Tell me if I should include any dates or details you think are missing."

### Example B — a "sorry we're behind" email

> ROLE: "You are a construction office manager."
> TASK: "Draft a polite email telling a client their patio pour is delayed one week due to rain."
> CONTEXT: "Job was scheduled for Thursday; forecast is heavy rain Wed–Thu; we'll pour Monday
> if weather holds. We reschedule, never cancel. Client was already anxious about timing."
> FORMAT: "Short, apologetic but confident, give the new date and a phone number."
> CHECK: "Keep it under 90 words."

### Example C — a site note → clean record

> ROLE: "You are a project note-taker."
> TASK: "Turn my messy field notes into a clean job log."
> CONTEXT: "Here are my notes: [paste your notes]. Job = Henderson foundation, 8-11am, crew 4,
> rebar in place, inspector due Friday, one truck late 40 min, need extra 2 yds of 4000 mix."
> FORMAT: "A dated log with sections: What happened / Crew / Materials / Issues / Next steps."
> CHECK: "Ask me anything that's unclear instead of guessing."

## Part 3 — Summarize Anything (Emails, Contracts, Specs)

AI's single best everyday trick: **paste it in, get the short version.** This saves you the
20-minute read on the 6-email chain from the GC or the 30-page spec sheet.

> "Summarize this in 4 bullet points: what's being asked, any deadlines, any costs, and anything
> that needs my action. If a number is missing, write 'MISSING' in its place. Use plain English."

**Rules of thumb:**
- Paste emails, RFPs, spec excerpts, scope letters, lease/contract pages — anything text-based.
- For PDFs/Word docs, upload the file to ChatGPT or Claude and ask the same thing.
- Always ask it to flag **action items** and **dates** — that's where you get your day back.
- Don't paste a whole contract to a free tool. Redact names/addresses first (see Lesson 5).

## Part 4 — Marketing & Social Posts Without the Blank Page

Concrete work is local. AI is great at turning one good job photo + a few facts into a post.

> "You are a social media writer for a concrete contractor. Write a Facebook post about this job:
> stamped concrete patio, 1,100 sq ft, new homeowner, finished last week. Include the contractor's
> name (Lakeview Concrete). Tone: friendly, local, proud of the work. End with 'Call for a free
> quote' and a phone number placeholder. Keep it under 120 words."

Repeat for Google Business, before/after posts, and even a short ad. Feed it the same facts,
ask for a different format. One job = a whole month of posts.

> **Pro tip:** always review the post. AI doesn't know the job's backstory (the tricky access,
> the days of rain, the client who nearly cancelled). You add the truth, it adds the speed.

## Part 5 — Voice Notes (Dictate Between Sites)

Use your phone's AI app's voice feature to dictate while you're driving (hands-free) or walking a
site, then have AI clean it up.

> "Turn this voice note into a tidy email to the GC: [dictated text]. Keep my meaning, fix the
> grammar, keep it short."

Try it: speak a rambling 60-second update about a job, then paste the transcript in and watch it
become a clean paragraph. This one habit saves most contractors 2–3 hours a week.

## Try It Yourself (5 minutes)

1. Open your chosen AI tool.
2. Write ONE email your business actually needs this week — a bid cover, a delay notice, or a
   follow-up — using the 5-part prompt.
3. Read the draft. Change anything that's wrong or that "doesn't sound like you."
4. Save your 5-part prompt somewhere (Notes app is fine) — you'll reuse it.
