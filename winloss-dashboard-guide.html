1 Getting started

Open winloss-dashboard.html in Chrome, Edge, or Firefox. No installation is required; the only external dependency is the Chart.js library loaded from a CDN, so an internet connection is needed once when the page first loads.

The dashboard ships with 140 seeded demo deals so every panel, chart, and recommendation is populated the moment it opens. Use Reset Sample (top right) at any time to restore the demo data.

2 Layout at a glance

Left rail — Data Ingestion & Filtering panel contains every control that shapes the analysis:

  • Pursuit & scope filters (industry, region, deal size, team)
  • Deal ingestion (outcome, date range, CSV import)
  • Competitor pricing intelligence box
  • Customer deal requirements box
  • Scenario simulator slider

Main view renders, in order: five KPI cards → trend and pricing charts → duration / TCV / cycle charts → benchmark and component charts → root-cause charts → competitor table + scenario card → insight feed.

Everything recalculates automatically when any control changes — there is no "Apply" button.

3 Filtering the view

  • Chips (industry, region, TCV bracket, team): click to toggle. Multiple chips can be active; the filter matches any of the selected values. If no chip is selected, all values are included.
  • Outcome: All / Won / Lost narrows to a single outcome.
  • Date range: use the two date fields to limit by close date.
Tip: combine filters to compare one pursuit team across regions, or one vertical across deal-size brackets. The KPI cards, all charts, and the insight feed update together.

The Competitor Intelligence table (main view) then shows each competitor’s typical discount, share of internal losses, and the note — useful for calibrating the discount ceiling and counter-messaging.

6 Customer requirements / RFx notes

List the solution elements customers are asking for in active pursuits. The insight engine reads this text and flags components that appear in 60%+ of your deals but are not mentioned in the requirements — i.e. potential missing service elements to add as optional line items before you scope short.

7 Scenario simulator (discount ceiling)

Drag the Proposed discount ceiling slider (0–25%) to model a pricing guardrail across the current filter set. The scenario card shows:

  • Won deals currently quoting above the ceiling
  • Projected margin uplift if discounts were capped (each point of discount removed restores ~0.5 points of margin)
  • Price-driven losses that fall inside the band — candidates you might recover with value-led packaging instead of discounting

8 Reading the KPI cards

KPIWhat it tells you
Overall Win RateShare of won deals in scope, with the ▲/▼ delta vs the unfiltered dataset.
Avg Deal Cycle (Won)Average and median cycle length of won deals — your closing cadence.
Avg Margin Won vs LostMargin achieved on won deals compared with the margin quoted on lost deals.

9 Reading the charts

  • Win Rate Trend — quarterly win rate (line) vs won/lost volume (bars). Look for persistent dips that align with pricing or team changes.
  • Outcome vs Pricing Variance — win rate per discount bucket (bars) plus the average margin achieved (line). This is the chart that sets the pricing guardrail.
  • Win Rate by Contract Duration — whether longer or shorter terms convert better in your current scope.
  • Win Rate by Deal Size (TCV) — which bracket is the sweet spot; low-volume brackets may just need more data.
  • Avg Deal Cycle Length — won vs lost cycle times; a big gap is a red flag for deal hygiene.
  • Benchmark vs External Baselines — your win rate and margin by region vs the industry baseline.
  • Solution Component Attach — radar of win rate where each service is attached vs how often it appears.
  • Lost-Deal Root Causes — the internal mix of why deals are lost (doughnut).
  • Loss Mix vs Benchmark — your loss reasons compared against the external baseline, so you can see where you deviate from the market.

10 The insight engine & its rules

Recommendations are computed live from the filtered data. Each card shows a severity color (red = high, amber = medium, green = low) and the reasoning behind it.

RuleWhat it detects
Pricing ceilingThe discount bucket where win rate peaks — the suggested cap for active pursuits.
Root cause attackThe most common loss reason in scope and a call to build a playbook for it.
Missing elementLowest-attach component — bundle it by default in proposals.
Geography gapRegions underperforming the external baseline by the largest margin.
Cadence dragWhen lost deals cycle much longer than won deals — sets a stage-gate warning.
Systemic price sensitivityWhen price causes more than 45% of losses — time to rebuild value-selling thresholds.
Proposal coverageComponents attaching to 60%+ of deals but absent from your RFx notes.
Insights are heuristics, not forecasts. Treat them as a review checklist to bring to a deal review, then validate against the underlying data on the charts.

11 Where the benchmarks come from

The external baseline (EX_BENCH in the code) represents sector-level figures — win rate by region and industry, a typical discount band, and the typical loss mix — drawn from managed-services market win/loss survey pools. You can edit these numbers directly in the JavaScript (search for EX_BENCH) to use your own market data.

12 Appearance (light / dark)

Use the theme toggle at the top right to switch between the light and dark palettes. Charts, grids, tables, and tooltips recolor automatically. The dashboard defaults to the light theme.

Internally each deal is a record like this:

  "id": "DL-1042",
  "iso": "2024-06-18",
  "quarter": "24-Q2",
  "region": "EMEA",
  "industry": "Government",
  "team": "Atlas",
  "bracket": "$1M\u2013$5M",
  "tcv": 2400000,
  "discount": 11.2,          // discount vs standard rate (%)
  "margin": 26.4,            // quoted / achieved margin (%)
  "cycleDays": 71,           // deal cycle length (days)
  "contractMonths": 24,
  "outcome": "lost",
  "lossReason": "Price sensitivity",
  "competitor": "GloboTech",
  "components": ["Managed Services", "Data & Analytics"]
}

The demo data is generated deterministically (seed 520175) by genDeals() so every refresh reproduces the same patterns for repeatable demos.

14 Export / print protection

By design this build is locked to protect the author’s work: Export CSV is disabled (the button is shown greyed out), printing and Ctrl+P/Ctrl+S are blocked, and right-click (Save Image / context menu) is disabled. Importing data and Reset Sample still work for demos.

15 Suggested workflow

  1. Scope it. Select the region, vertical, and pursuit team you want to review.
  2. Load data. Import your CSV so the analysis reflects real deals, not the demo set.
  3. Spot the trend. Read the trend and pricing charts to see where win rate and margin are eroding.
  4. Benchmark. Compare your numbers against the external baseline to separate controllable issues from market norms.
  5. Simulate. Test a discount ceiling to protect margin.
  6. Act. Work through the insight feed as a review checklist and assign owners to each recommendation.
