Economic Value Estimation

Turn your deal's value into a defensible annual price — and the headroom to negotiate around it.

What is this tool?

This calculator applies Economic Value Estimation (EVE) — a value-based pricing method. Instead of pricing features, it prices the economic value our solution creates relative to the customer's next best alternative (competitor).

The logic: take the competitor's price, add the net value of our differentiation (the two value drivers you name, minus switching friction), and recommend a price that captures only 30–40% of that net value — leaving the customer most of the benefit so the deal is a clear win.

How to use it

  • Fill in the Deal Inputs panel on the left — everything recalculates live.
  • Read the Target Price Range for the recommended annual list price.
  • Use Negotiation Headroom to see how far you can discount before losing the 30% floor, and the value split per dollar.
  • Check the 5-Year Forecast for the multi-year ROI story and feature-1 sensitivity for negotiation prep.
  • Save any input set as a scenario preset to compare deals or revisit later.

What the buttons do

  • 🔗 Link — copies a shareable URL that opens this exact scenario (no install needed by the receiver).
  • ▤ CSV — downloads every number as a spreadsheet file.
  • ⧉ Share — copies the summary as Markdown to paste into Slack, Notion, or email.
  • ⓘ About — toggles this explanation panel.
  • Theme (moon/sun icon) — switches light / dark, remembered on this browser.
  • ⬇ Export PDF — downloads a formatted pricing summary matching the current theme.

Assumptions to validate: the two annual value-driver figures you enter (name them in Feature 1 / Feature 2 labels) and the onboarding cost. Confirm actuals with the customer before quoting.

Capture ladder — anchor before you negotiate

Capture = the share of net differentiation value your price keeps. Each row prices the deal at a different capture level so you can see, at a glance, how the list price and the customer's retained value move together. Guide: 30–40% captures the sweet spot — you make a premium on the value you create, while the customer keeps the majority of the benefit (that's what makes the deal an obvious win). Below 25% you're leaving money on the table; above 50% the economics start to look like your price, not their value — expect hard pushback and churn risk. In negotiation, this ladder is your anchor: start near the high end, and every concession you trade is a step down the ladder you can frame in dollars the customer keeps.

    ◈ 5-Year Forecast & Sensitivityi

    What is NDV? Net Differentiation Value = Gross Differentiation Value (your two feature values combined) minus one-time switching friction. It's the economic value you deliver beyond the competitor — and the size of the pie you price against.
    Why Yr 1 is lower: Year 1 deducts the onboarding friction, so its bar is shorter. Years 2+ carry no friction, so NDV is the full value. Per year, captured premium = NDV × your value-capture %, and customer value kept = NDV minus that premium.
